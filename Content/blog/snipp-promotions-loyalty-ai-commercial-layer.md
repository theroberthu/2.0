## NON-NEGOTIABLE: MEASUREMENT GUARDRAIL

Receipt validation MAY establish:
- that a qualifying SKU was purchased
- that promotion rules were satisfied
- that a receipt was valid

It does NOT establish:
- which AI conversation caused the purchase
- whether the AI interaction influenced the purchase
- whether the promotion was incremental
- whether the shopper would have purchased anyway

**Never describe this as closed-loop AI attribution.** The protecting sentences
are "confirming a purchase is not attributing it", "Snipp has not shown that a
validated receipt can be tied back to a specific conversation", and "A rebate
claimed by someone who was always going to buy is a cost, not an outcome."

---

## NON-NEGOTIABLE: OPT-IN LIMITATION

The third commercial layer currently depends on:
- plugin / connector installation
- tool invocation
- platform directory availability
- shopper adoption

**Do not present it as automatically available across ChatGPT or Claude
conversations.** The protecting conclusion is "That makes owned incentive data a
layer that currently exists by invitation, not by default."

Directory availability is Snipp's claim. Both directory URLs returned 403 to
automated checks at publication and could not be independently confirmed.

---

## NON-NEGOTIABLE: COST FRAMING

The owned-incentive distinction is about the **commercial role of the data**, not
whether the brand pays anything. "No additional platform fees" and "no new
marketplace commissions" are Snipp's claims about this distribution route, and
the article attributes them to Snipp. **Do not imply participating brands incur
no costs of any kind**; brands may still pay Snipp. The article describes the
offer as "not conventional paid media inventory", not as free.

## REVISIONS (Editorial Board, applied before publication)

1. Organic-distribution sentence softened. "Organic answers reach everyone who
   asks. Ads reach whoever the ad system selects." -> "Organic recommendations
   can surface without a shopper installing a separate brand tool. Ads reach
   users according to the platform's ad-delivery system." The invocation clause
   became "and then only when that capability is invoked". Conclusion preserved.
   Reason: organic answers do not literally reach everyone, and ad reach is the
   platform's decision rather than a selection the article can characterize.

2. Owned-incentive distinction refined. "It is not a recommendation... It is not
   an ad, because nobody bought placement in the answer." -> "It is not an
   organic recommendation, because the brand supplied the offer. And it is not
   conventional paid media inventory." Reason: the original implied no payment
   anywhere in the chain.

## EVIDENCE NOTES

- Snipp names **no customers** and reports **no performance results**. Verified
  by reading the full release.
- Unsourced statistics in the release: 42% of consumers used AI to shop in the
  past month; $385B by 2030 (PPC Land: "cites the top of the range without the
  floor"); 86% say rewards influence shopping; nearly one-third cite enrollment
  friction; 28% abandon programs requiring another app. PPC Land also notes a
  Morgan Stanley survey put AI purchasing at roughly 23%, about half Snipp's
  figure. **None of these appear in the article as facts.**
- Verification approval: the release says "approved by OpenAI and Claude". Claude
  is a product; the article says "each platform" and attributes it to Snipp.
- "Purchase can occur wherever the shopper normally buys" is an inference from
  retailer-agnostic receipt validation, labelled as Robert's reading.
- "Only when that capability is invoked" reflects connector architecture, not a
  Snipp statement.
- OpenAI ads separate from answers: OpenAI Help Center and the 2026-05-05 OpenAI
  post, verified earlier in the week.
- Walmart sponsored prompts is stated from prior TRH coverage, not re-verified
  against a Walmart primary source.
